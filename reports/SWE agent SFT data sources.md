# Ranking 2026's permissive Python repo-repair corpora

**Four sources carry a Python-first repository-repair SFT campaign as of late October 2026: `nebius/SWE-rebench` plus its OpenHands trajectory set for the executable backbone, `nvidia/Open-SWE-Traces` for scale and the only `resolved` label on a million-row corpus, `SWE-bench/SWE-smith-py` plus its `xml` trajectories for the cheapest executable environments under a clean MIT grant, and `SWE-Gym` as a provably SWE-bench-disjoint held-out cohort.** Everything else in a 875-dataset sweep either re-trajectories the same handful of task pools, is localization/competitive-programming/eval data misfiled under a "SWE" name, or carries a licence defect. The verified, independently computed Python ceiling is roughly **80,000 executable task instances across ~3,500–4,500 distinct repositories**, of which only **~13,785 nebius instances and ~39,471 usable SWE-smith-py instances are simultaneously Python, image-backed or install-scripted, and non-degenerate**; measured supervised assistant-token volume for a success-filtered single-serialization blend lands near **0.5 billion tokens verified and 1–3 billion if the NVIDIA estimates hold**. The research also surfaced **at least a dozen quantified contradictions between dataset cards and the actual parquet files** — SWE-smith's prose undercounts its own repos by 94, SWE-smith-py silently ships 11,437 rows with an empty problem statement, `nebius/SWE-bench-extra`'s prose overcounts by 39 rows, and NVIDIA's own paper abstract is off by 21,269 trajectories against its own HuggingFace artefact. **Licence exposure is the sharpest risk and runs the opposite way from expectations: nothing carries a non-commercial clause, but fifteen-plus genuinely useful artifacts declare no licence at all, one card explicitly forbids use as training data, and no card on any dataset settles the rights in the upstream repository source code that every patch and trajectory embeds verbatim.**

Throughout this report, **[verified]** marks a fact the researchers computed themselves — DuckDB over the pinned parquet revision, the HuggingFace size/statistics APIs, or Docker registry manifest reads, all executed 2026-10-28/29 UTC. **(claim)** marks publisher prose from a dataset card, blog or paper that was *not* independently reproduced. The distinction is load-bearing: in several cases below the two disagree, and where they do, the computed figure wins.

**Rank 1 and 2 together are the campaign's spine.** They share an `instance_id` join, and the trajectory set's 6,306 distinct instances line up almost exactly with the 6,542-row `filtered` split that carries Docker images — strong evidence the rollouts were run over the image-backed subset with ~236 instances dropped ([nebius/SWE-rebench](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-rebench)). Tool-call arguments are stored as a **JSON-encoded string**, not a struct, and a per-row `tools` column embeds the full schema, so prompts can be reconstructed without the sidecar file [verified].

**Rank 3 is the volume play but carries the largest unmeasured hole.** `Open-SWE-Traces` ships four configs — `v2.0` 489,109 rows, `v1.2` 235,391, `v1.1` 263,226, `v1.0` 151,219 — each split five ways by harness (`claudecode`, `opencode`, `openhands`, `sweagent`, `minisweagent`), with teachers rotating by version from MiniMax-M2.5 and Qwen3.5-122B through DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731, Qwen3.8-27B and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ([Open-SWE-Traces](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Open-SWE-Traces)). It is the only corpus in this inventory where `language='Python' AND resolved=1` is expressible as a filter. Nobody ran that filter: at 98 GB across 487 files in four configs the column-projected scan exceeded the researcher's budget, so **the Python resolved-row count is a genuine unknown**, and its distinct-issue and distinct-repo counts are likewise uncomputed.

**The critical structural warning for anyone blending ranks 1, 3, 4 and 7:** these corpora are not independent. `Open-SWE-Traces` draws its instances *exclusively* from `AweAI-Team/Scale-SWE` and `nebius/SWE-rebench-V2`; `SWE-Hero` is 53.1% `nebius/SWE-rebench` by construction with **100% exact `instance_id` match on that slice** [verified]; `Lego-X/SWE-Lego-Real-Data` and `SWE-Review-Traj` are built on `nebius/SWE-rebench`; `AlienKevin/SWE-ZERO-12M-trajectories` is seeded from `nebius/SWE-rebench-V2-PRs`; `ricdomolm/mini-coder-trajs-400k` runs on SWE-smith; and `nvidia/SWE-Zero` draws on SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, `internlm/SWE-Fixer-Train-110K` and SWE-rebench. **Row counts are therefore never additive.** What these sets add is teacher and scaffold diversity over a small shared instance pool, which is real value but a different kind of value from new tasks.

**Tool-schema incompatibility is a real integration cost, not a footnote.** The nebius OpenHands set ships five tools; NVIDIA's SWE-Hero and SWE-Zero ship a **strict subset of four** — identical `execute_bash` / `think` / `finish` / `str_replace_editor` definitions, **no `task_tracker`** ([SWE-Hero tools.json](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/SWE-Hero-openhands-trajectories/raw/main/tools.json)). Blending them means either dropping `task_tracker` turns from the nebius data or accepting that the model sees a tool in some system prompts and not others. SWE-smith's three splits are worse: the `tool` / `xml` / `ticks` names are the *only* evidence that they are three action serializations, the authors document the distinction nowhere, and **a model trained on one will not accept another without re-templating** — yet all 4,954 trajectories of the published SWE-agent-LM-32B recipe live in `xml` and **zero** live in `ticks` [verified].

**Trajectory shape, where it was measured.** SWE-Hero rows have exactly one `system` and one `user` message each — the issue statement is a single user turn with no multi-turn user interaction — ending in exactly one `finish` call, at **~58.8 assistant tool calls and a mean 118.57 messages per trajectory** [verified over shard 0], with the viewer's 22,500-row sample reporting min 49 / max 201 / mean 123.77 messages. That 201-message ceiling matches NVIDIA's own SWE-RL docs stating the OpenHands loop runs "up to 200 turns" ([NVIDIA Nemotron SWE-RL docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/nemotron/latest/nemotron/super3/rl/swe.html)). SWE-Hero's rollout design is **3 per issue** (11,066 issues have exactly 3) [verified]. Nebius is adaptive by contrast: mode 7, mean 10.6, **max 34** rollouts per issue, and the **6,003 `max iteration: 100` exit statuses empirically confirm the card's 100-turn cap** [verified].

**Evaluator-only material, which must be stripped from prompts.** Both nebius task corpora ship gold patch and verification test patch on **100% of rows**, plus explicit F2P/P2P lists (V1 adds F2F/P2F). For V1 the safe prompt fields are `problem_statement`, `repo`, `base_commit`, `install_config` and `image_name`; `patch`, `test_patch`, all four test-ID lists and `meta.llm_score` must be withheld, and `hints_text` — defined on the card as "comments made on the issue prior to the creation of the solution PR's first commit" — is a leakage hazard of a different kind because maintainer discussion often contains the fix. V2 additionally requires excluding `interface` and `meta.llm_metadata.code`/`reasoning`, which are LLM descriptions of the changed code. **No nebius card warns that any field is evaluator-only**; this is inference from field semantics. `SWE-bench-Live` is the one publisher that states the protocol outright: "During a rollout, the agent may access only the `problem_statement` field… It must not access any other fields, such as `hint`, `FAIL_TO_PASS`, or `test_patch`" ([SWE-bench-Live card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench-Live/SWE-bench-Live/raw/main/README.md)). On the NVIDIA side, **`Open-SWE-Traces` is the only set shipping a gold patch** (`metadata.reference_patch.patch`), and **none of the NVIDIA sets ship verification tests at all** — no `FAIL_TO_PASS`, `PASS_TO_PASS` or `test_patch` field exists anywhere in that family, so a verifier must be rebuilt by joining `instance_id` back to the upstream collection.

## Fourteen quantified contradictions between cards and data

Every figure below is a case where a publisher's prose and the actual artifact disagree, with the gap measured. These are not rounding differences; several would silently corrupt a training budget.

**SWE-smith is the worst offender, with four mutually inconsistent instance counts in circulation.** The `SWE-bench/SWE-smith` card prose says "a training dataset of **50137** task instances from **128** GitHub repositories" while its own YAML metadata says `num_examples: 59136` and the parquet files contain **59,136 rows over 222 distinct repos** [verified] — the prose is wrong by roughly 9,000 instances and **94 repositories** ([SWE-smith card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-smith/raw/main/README.md)). The paper abstract repeats the stale "50k instances from 128 repositories" ([arXiv:2504.21798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21798)), and the GitHub README offers a third figure, "**52k** task instances" and "**250+** environments" ([SWE-smith README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SWE-bench/SWE-smith/main/README.md)). Only the per-language card is right: `SWE-smith-py`'s "50908 task instances from 131 GitHub repositories" **matches the data exactly** [verified].

**SWE-smith-py's 22.47% empty-problem-statement rate is undocumented anywhere and is the most consequential undisclosed fact in the inventory.** 11,437 of 50,908 rows ship with a completely empty `problem_statement` [verified], and emptiness is strongly strategy-correlated — 0% for `lm_rewrite`, 0.8% for the PR-mirror family, but 19.5–35.4% for the procedural and combination families — consistent with the issue-generation pass having been run selectively. The usable Python issue-driven pool is therefore **39,471 rows, not 50,908**, unless you pay to regenerate issue text yourself.

**SWE-smith-trajectories' card claims 5,017 trajectories; the repo's own ID list has 4,954; the published splits are five times larger than either.** The card says it "contains the **5017** trajectories we fine-tuned Qwen 2.5 Coder Instruct on" and tags `size_categories: 1K<n<10K`, yet the three splits hold 24,100 / 26,076 / 25,826 rows ([SWE-smith-trajectories card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SWE-bench/SWE-smith-trajectories/raw/main/README.md)). The repo's `traj_ids_swealm32b.txt` lists **4,954 unique traj_ids**, all of them `resolved=true` and all in `xml` ([traj_ids_swealm32b.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SWE-bench/SWE-smith/main/docs/assets/traj_ids_swealm32b.txt)). **The 63-trajectory difference is unexplained.** The card also names a single teacher, "SWE-agent + Claude 3.7 Sonnet", when the data contains three [verified]. And the repo carries **eight orphan parquet files totalling 1,052,703,887 bytes referenced by no `configs` entry** — a byte-for-byte duplicate of the `xml` split on every statistic checked — so a naive `snapshot_download` pulls ~1 GB of redundant data.

**Nebius's Docker-image claim overstates what the dataset exposes.** The V1 card says "We've released 7,500 pre-built Docker images… You do not need to build them yourself", but **only 6,542 of 21,336 rows carry an image name** while the `swerebench/` namespace holds **8,127 repositories** [verified] — three different numbers for the same thing. Separately, `nebius/SWE-bench-extra`'s prose claims "6,415 Issue-Pull Request pairs" against an actual **6,376 rows** [verified, −39]. And the trajectory card documents the field `pred_passes_gen_test` (singular) where the parquet column is `pred_passes_gen_tests` (plural), while omitting the per-row `tools` column entirely [verified].

**NVIDIA's paper and its own artifacts disagree by 21,269 trajectories.** The SWE-ZERO/SWE-HERO abstract claims a release of "**300k** SWE-ZERO and **13k** SWE-HERO trajectories" ([arXiv:2604.01496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01496)); the HuggingFace cards and viewer report **318,115 and 34,269** [verified]. The HF artefacts were last modified 2026-05-05 and 2026-05-08, after the paper, so treat the paper figures as superseded. `Open-SWE-Traces` has the same problem internally: its `data_distribution.md` reports v2.0 at 168,824 trajectories and v1.2 at 235,396, against viewer counts of **489,109 and 235,391** — the v2.0 gap is because the config globs reasoning-effort subdirectories (`thinking-low`, `thinking-medium`) the table omits ([data_distribution.md](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Open-SWE-Traces/raw/main/data_distribution.md)). `Nemotron-SWE-v1` ships **three conflicting counts** — viewer 24,875, card table 51,029, card prose "59k agent trajectories" — and `Nemotron-RL-Agentic-SWE-Pivot-v1`'s card claims 6,436 samples with 2 top-level features against a viewer reporting **50,308 rows and 11 columns**.

**Smaller but real.** R2E-Gym's paper abstract claims "more than **8.7K** tasks" against 8,101 published rows ([arXiv:2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)), and V1 ships **1,192 rows (14.7%) with an empty `problem_statement`** where the Subset has zero — so the V1→Subset relationship is partly a quality filter nobody documents. `R2E-Gym-Lite`'s `dev_100pr_v1` and `dev_100pr_v2` splits have **byte-identical parquet sizes**, almost certainly the same rows under two names. `SWE-Gym-Lite`'s HF card says 230 instances while the project README says 234 ([SWE-Gym README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SWE-Gym/SWE-Gym/main/README.md)). `SWE-bench/SWE-smith-php` contains exactly **one row** — a placeholder, not a dataset. And `SWE-Gym-Raw` reports 64,689 rows over only **62,041 distinct instance_ids** [verified].

**One contradiction is between the researchers themselves and remains unresolved.** Reading the same `nebius/SWE-rebench` parquet glob, one researcher measured **21,336 rows** and the other **27,878 physical rows with 21,336 distinct `instance_id`s** — i.e. ~6.5k duplicate ids, most plausibly from the `test` and `filtered` splits both being globbed, since all 6,542 `filtered` rows also appear in `test` [verified]. **Anyone pinning this dataset should read splits explicitly rather than globbing `data/*.parquet`.**

### Gaps the researchers could not close

Several questions remain genuinely open and should not be papered over. **Per-trajectory turn-count distributions for the nebius OpenHands set were never computed** — the query hit HuggingFace's anonymous rate limit (HTTP 429), so the card's "average 64.3 turns" stays a claim, corroborated on the max-100 figure only. **The V1↔V2 Python overlap was never computed**, so the 28,579-instance union is an upper bound, not a deduplicated count; the join key must be `(repo, base_commit)` or `(repo, PR#)` because V1's `instance_id` scheme (`owner__repo-PR`) differs from V2's. **`Open-SWE-Traces`' Python-and-resolved count, distinct issues and distinct repos are all unmeasured.** **`SWE-Zero`'s distinct issue and repo counts are unmeasured** — only the 118,092-issue claim and an unverified third-party mirror's "628 repo classes" exist. Whether **SWE-Hero rows were execution-filtered is only implied by the paper's framing** of SWE-HERO as the "execution-backed" stage and is never asserted on the card. **Exact OpenHands versions for SWE-Hero and SWE-Zero are not stated anywhere**, in contrast to `Open-SWE-Traces` v1.0 (v0.53.0), nebius (v0.54.0) and SWE-Lego (v0.53.0). **No SWE-agent version is stated for SWE-smith-trajectories** on the card, in the README, or in the paper. **`SALT-NLP/SWE-chat`** (`202b071f18e03c79df5a7287565ce2ebc5ee7756`, 47.0 GB, 21,675 files, `odc-by`) is **gated** — viewer returns HTTP 401, raw README returns "Access to dataset SALT-NLP/SWE-chat is restricted" — making it the single largest unresolved unknown in the inventory. **`laion/openswe-harbor-tasks`** (45,320 tasks) has **no README at all** ("Entry not found") and no licence. `ByteDance-Seed/Multi-SWE-bench_trajs`' row count is unobtainable (viewer HTTP 500), and all three `AweAI-Team` trajectory counts return 0 viewer rows, making 71k / ~41k / 11k / 34k publisher claims from card text and repo names only. Finally, **every token figure in this report is a character-count extrapolation, not a tokenizer run** — SWE-Hero's is extrapolated from 1 of 14 shards, SWE-smith's carries ±50% shard variance on top of ±30% from the chars-per-token assumption.

## Licence risk runs backwards: nothing bans commerce, fifteen artifacts grant nothing

**The expected risk — a non-commercial or competition-use clause — does not exist in this corpus class.** Across every card read, in three independent sweeps, **no non-commercial clause, no competition restriction, and no SWE-bench "evaluation only" clause appears anywhere.** The actual risks are three different things.

**First: fifteen-plus genuinely useful artifacts declare no licence at all, which is legally worse than a restrictive one.** On the SWE-bench side that covers `SWE-smith-java` (`1172df9919a8232e066562dfdb6e0193156de0c4`), `-js` (`d6aeb41ba9fb045c39e1c6d2322a8f08ee31cef4`), `-ts` (`c243d86f112d01b8507d311af912668aa1d74449`), `-rs` (`1ae20d63edd611309d86133466247e3a9723a27d`), `-cpp` (`b1554a5f12603c825f9b48b361b9b97032347e48`) and `-php` (`6b5da17f1e3543fb2ed5d151a6f4972a457a8c36`) — almost certainly a drafting oversight, since their `py`/`go` siblings are MIT and the source repo is MIT ("MIT License / Copyright (c) 2025 John Yang"), but as published they carry no grant. On the SWE-Gym side: `SWE-Gym-Lite`, `SWE-Gym-Raw`, `OpenHands-Sampled-Trajectories`, `OpenHands-Verifier-Trajectories` and `MoatlessTools-Sampled-Trajectories`. On the R2E-Gym side: `R2E-Gym-Lite`, `R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories`, `R2EGym-Verifier-Trajectories` and `R2EGym-TestingAgent-SFT-Trajectories`. Across the 2026 releases: `Lego-X/LegoFlow-SWE`, `Lego-X/Terminal-Lego-Traj-Deepseek-V3-2-15k`, `zai-org/SWE-Dev-train`, all three `AweAI-Team` Scale-SWE sets, `OpenHands/CodeScout_Training_Rollouts`, `OpenHands/SWE-rebench-code-search`, `hamishivi/swerl-tmax-15k`, `TMaxxx/TMax-15K-Harbor`, both `laion/openswe-*` sets, `II-Vietnam/Agentic-Multi-SWE-RL`, `whitecircle/...-successful-traces`, `swesynth/SWE-Synth_Moatless-SFT-Trajectories` and `ScaleAI/SWE-bench_Pro`. Even `SWE-bench/SWE-smith-envs` — the Docker build artifacts — returns **`license: null`** from the GitHub API ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/SWE-bench/SWE-smith-envs)). **`R2E-Gym/R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories` is the highest-risk artifact in the whole inventory**: its README is 352 bytes of auto-generated YAML and nothing else — no licence, no teacher model, no scaffold, no success criterion, no provenance — and its 3,231 rows contain **no instance identifier**, so it cannot even be decontaminated against SWE-bench Verified by joining on an id ([R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/R2E-Gym/R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories/raw/main/README.md)).

**Second: one card forbids use as training data outright, in direct contradiction to its own metadata.** `rmems/long-horizon-coding-trajectories` declares `license: apache-2.0` in its metadata while the card body states: "Hosted frontier-model outputs are research-only inputs under project policy… `intended_use: research_only`, `project_training_policy: blocked`. **Not training data for any model-weight update.**" It also carries a `research-only` tag ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/rmems/long-horizon-coding-trajectories/raw/main/README.md)). **Exclude it.** Three further field-of-use flags matter. `nvidia/Llama-Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset` (`ab2a40d258a6a4d9d4c277d702aeea445081766c`) is the **only clause found that imposes conditions on models trained on the data**: "If this dataset is used to create, train, fine tune, or otherwise improve an AI model, which is distributed or made available, such AI model may be subject to redistribution and use requirements in the Llama 3.1 Community License Agreement and Llama 3.3 Community License Agreement" — and it also mixes in ODC-BY Wildchat prompts and **CC-BY-SA StackOverflow prompts**, so a per-sample filter on its `license` column is advisable ([README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Llama-Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset/raw/main/README.md)). `nebius/SWE-agent-trajectories` carries the analogous notice: "if you intend to use the outputs of these models, you must comply with the Llama 3.1 License", because its traces were generated partly with Llama3-70B-Instruct. `nvidia/Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v3.5` (`ad64129216649f86179a759a6df65d0975c5df07`) states "**This dataset is for commercial uses only**" — taken literally this *excludes* academic use, the inverse of every other NVIDIA card's "ready for commercial/non-commercial use". It is most likely a drafting error, but it is the published text and deserves legal review. And `nvidia/Nemotron-SFT-Agentic-v4` (`a4593a26ea83a700035bbe0ec1edc9ef3810669d`) sits under **`openmdw-1.1`**, a different legal regime from CC-BY; since it is not repo-repair data, the simplest mitigation is to exclude it. Bespoke or unspecified terms also attach to `JetBrains-Research/SWE-TaskFlow-*` (`license: other`, no `license_name` or `license_link`), `FineEnvs/repo2rlenv-*` (`license: other`, `license_name: mixed-source-licenses`, pointing at a per-dataset `LICENSES.md` nobody read), and `ByteDance-Seed/Multi-SWE-RL`, whose metadata says `other` while the card says CC0 "subject to any intellectual property rights… owned by Bytedance" and enumerates per-repository licences including GPL, LGPL, MPL and AGPL projects such as `php/php-src`, `redis/redis` and `bitcoin/bitcoin` ([Multi-SWE-RL card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ByteDance-Seed/Multi-SWE-RL/raw/main/README.md)). The **OpenMDW-1.1 text itself was never read**, so its restrictive clauses cannot be quoted here.

**Third, and most important: no card licence settles the rights in the upstream repository source code that every patch, problem statement, trajectory and Docker image embeds verbatim.** Nebius says so itself, in identical text on all four main cards: "The dataset is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license. **However, please respect the license of each specific repository on which a particular instance is based.** To facilitate this, the license of each repository at the time of the commit is provided for every instance" ([SWE-rebench README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-rebench/raw/main/README.md)). NVIDIA says the same on `Open-SWE-Traces`: "Each user is responsible for checking the content of datasets and the applicable licenses and determining if suitable for the intended use." A CC-BY-4.0 or MIT card plausibly covers the compilation, the LLM-generated annotations and the install configs; it cannot relicense the `patch`, `test_patch` and `problem_statement` fields, which are verbatim derivatives of upstream code and issue text.

The good news is that the per-row licence data exists and the Python slices are overwhelmingly permissive. `nebius/SWE-rebench`'s 21,336-row `test` split is **41.54% MIT, 23.29% Apache-2.0, 17.43% BSD-3-Clause**, with a long permissive tail (Zope, MIT-CMU, ISC, Unlicense, CC0, PSF, AFL) and — notably — **zero GPL/AGPL/LGPL strings in 56 distinct free-text values** [verified]. The unresolvable residue is **532 NULL rows (2.49%)** plus **1,288 rows** of bare "BSD"/"BSD License" that cannot be mapped to SPDX from the dataset alone. V2 is cleaner in format but dirtier in substance: 16 SPDX-ish values, **348 NULLs (1.085%)**, and **5,038 rows (15.7%) carrying `custom-check-github`** — a placeholder meaning "we did not resolve this, go look at the repo", which neither card defines. In the **Python slice specifically, `custom-check-github` is 1,706 of 7,243 rows (23.6%)**, and **all 54 AGPL-3.0 and 5 GPL-3.0 V2 rows are non-Python** and trivially excludable [verified]. NVIDIA restricted collection upstream — "We use MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD-2-Claude [sic], and BSD-3-Clause licenses only" — and `SWE-Hero`'s observed per-row distribution (22,500-row partial sample) is BSD-3-Clause 10,666 / Apache-2.0 6,179 / MIT 5,320 / BSD-2-Clause 333 / MIT-0 2. **The operational conclusion: retain the per-row `license` column through the entire training pipeline so you can produce Apache-2.0 NOTICE and BSD attribution, and so you can exclude licence classes later without re-downloading.** For SWE-smith (131 upstream repos), SWE-Gym (11) and R2E-Gym (13) no per-row licence field exists at all, and R2E-Gym's set includes **`orange3`, which is GPL-3.0** — an Apache-2.0 card cannot relicense GPL-3.0 source appearing verbatim in a patch or inside a published image. **Enumerating those 155 upstream licences is required follow-up work that no publisher has done.**

**A fourth layer nobody addresses at all: teacher-model terms of service.** `SWE-smith-trajectories` is 73% Claude 3.7 Sonnet and 24% Claude 3.5 Sonnet output [verified]; `SWE-Gym/OpenHands-SFT-Trajectories` is GPT-4o plus Claude 3.5 Sonnet (claim); `JetBrains-Research/agent-trajectories-swe-bench-test-minus-verified` uses gpt-5.2, gpt-5-mini and gemini-3-pro-preview; `SWE-TaskFlow-*` uses GPT-5.6 and Gemini 3.7; and `R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories` does not disclose whose outputs they are. An MIT card on a dataset does not waive the originating provider's output-use terms. **The open-weights-teacher sets — the nebius and NVIDIA Qwen3-Coder-480B lines, `Lego-X` (GLM-5.x), `AlienKevin` (mini-coder-1.7b), `ricdomolm` (Qwen3-Coder-30B) — are the clean ones on this axis.**

## Contamination: SWE-Gym is provably clean, R2E-Gym is 38.7% compromised, nebius V1 is unquantified

**No card in this inventory publishes a SWE-bench-Verified overlap count.** Not one. What exists instead is a mix of computed results, construction claims, and one third-party audit — and the computed results are sharply different per source.

**`SWE-Gym` is clean, and provably so.** Against `SWE-bench` (2,519 rows, 18 repos) and `SWE-bench_Verified` (500 rows, 12 repos), SWE-Gym's instance-id overlap is **zero** and — far more meaningfully — its **repository** overlap is also **zero** with both [verified]. Its 11 repos are deliberately disjoint from SWE-bench's 18. Since both use the same F2P/P2P schema, this is a meaningful result, not an artifact of incompatible id formats.

**`SWE-smith-py` is clean against Verified and nearly clean against SWE-bench.** Mapping its `swesmith/<owner>__<repo>.<commit8>` field back to `owner/repo` gives 131 repos, of which **4 appear in SWE-bench (dev+test)** — `marshmallow-code/marshmallow`, `pydicom/pydicom`, `sqlfluff/sqlfluff`, `pylint-dev/astroid` — and **0 appear in SWE-bench_Verified** [verified]. Three of those four are large in SWE-smith-py (astroid 1,770 rows, pydicom 1,328, sqlfluff 1,293), so dropping them costs ~4,400–5,300 rows and is worth doing if you report SWE-bench full or Lite numbers. Note that SWE-smith's instance ids are **structurally un-collidable** with SWE-bench's, so zero id overlap is trivial and is *not* evidence of cleanliness — only repo-level and commit-level checks matter.

**`R2E-Gym-V1` requires active decontamination and is the clear outlier.** **3,139 of 8,101 rows (38.7%)** come from `sympy` (2,331) and `matplotlib` (808), which are SWE-bench_Verified repos #2 and #4 by instance count [verified]. Worse, joining `R2E-Gym-V1.commit_hash` against `SWE-bench.base_commit` yields **54 exact matches** — the strongest contamination signal available anywhere in this inventory, because it means the agent may have trained on the exact tree state a Verified task starts from, including (via `expected_output_json`) the behaviour the Verified test asserts. Dropping `sympy` and `matplotlib` removes 38.7% of rows and leaves 4,962 from 11 repos. The repo-name match is on bare basenames rather than `owner/repo`, so 38.7% is formally an upper bound, but for `sympy` and `matplotlib` the canonical owner is near-certain.

**Nebius V1 is the significant unquantified exposure, and it is the backbone of the shortlist.** `nebius/SWE-rebench`'s card **contains no overlap or contamination statement whatsoever** — the full raw README mentions SWE-bench only as methodological lineage [verified by reading the complete README at rev `89cdfbab…`]. **No source anywhere quantifies V1's overlap with SWE-bench-Verified, SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym or SWE-smith.** V2, by contrast, carries an explicit warning: "❗ **Benchmark overlap:** We excluded repositories from the original SWE-bench, but did not explicitly filter other benchmarks. Before training, check the overlap analysis and task IDs and exclude tasks that overlap with your evaluation benchmarks" ([SWE-rebench-V2 README](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nebius/SWE-rebench-V2/raw/main/README.md)). The third-party audit it points at reports **348 of V2's 32,079 tasks (1.1%) matching a benchmark task** by PR and base commit, concentrated in multilingual benchmarks — SWE-PolyBench 239/2,110 (11.3%), Multi-SWE-bench Kotlin 14/105 (13.3%), SWE-PolyBench Verified 41/382 (10.7%), Multi-SWE-bench 83/1,632 (5.1%), SWE-bench Multilingual 12/300 (4.0%) — with **202 of the 239 PolyBench overlaps in a single JavaScript repo (`serverless/serverless`)** and all 12 Multilingual overlaps in three PHP repos ([agentleak README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/raimondasl/agentleak/main/README.md)). **For a Python-only team the quantified overlaps are therefore largely irrelevant** — they are concentrated in JS and PHP. The audit's most useful methodological finding is negative: a 13-gram text filter catches 347 of the 348 overlaps but **also flags 1,249 of the other 31,731 (≈4% false positives)**, and `SWE-rebench-V2-PRs`' 20 overlapping PRs share **no 8-gram** with the benchmarks because their problem statements are LLM-written. **Decontaminate on `(repo, base_commit)` and PR number, never on text n-grams.**

**The trajectory sets' decontamination posture lives in blog posts, not cards.** The nebius trajectory card carries **no overlap warning**; the authors' claim that they "explicitly excluded all SWE-bench Verified issues and repositories from our training data" and also excluded the SWE-rebench September instances appears **only in a blog post**, which also concedes they cannot determine whether the base models saw Verified during pretraining ([Nebius blog](https://nebius.com/blog/posts/openhands-trajectories-with-qwen3-coder-480b)). **NVIDIA's cards make no decontamination claim for SWE-Hero at all**, and the researchers did not run SWE-Hero against Verified or Scale-SWE. The 2026 cards generally document contamination *controls* rather than overlap counts: `SWE-bench-Live` promises monthly refresh for "contamination-free evaluation" with all instances created after 2024; `Lego-X/LegoFlow-SWE` documents an anti-hack prompt, **git history and remote removal before the agent starts**, and network restriction to the inference endpoint — while warning that the shipped `tasks-anti-hack/` variant "adds prompt instructions only"; and `Open-SWE-Traces` v1.0 was **pruned from 207k to 151k trajectories** after cleaning "trajectories exhibiting git hacking behaviors", which is reward hacking rather than benchmark overlap. One set is scoped clean by construction — `JetBrains-Research/agent-trajectories-swe-bench-test-minus-verified` is exactly SWE-bench Test minus Verified — **but its trajectories are oracle-informed**: a `gemini-3-pro-preview` router *with access to the ground-truth patch* picked the better of two candidate steps each turn, which is a different leakage axis entirely and makes those 1,785 rows unsuitable as a model of unaided agent behaviour.

**Designing the repository-disjoint held-out cohort.** Partition on `repo`, not `instance_id`, and ideally on *owner* — nebius V1 spans **3,468 repos across 2,653 owners** and the trajectory set **1,823 repos across 1,445 owners** [verified], so there is no small upstream set to negotiate with and plenty of room to carve a disjoint slice. The concrete recipe: hard-exclude the 18 SWE-bench repos and 12 Verified repos from all training splits; hard-exclude R2E-Gym's `sympy` and `matplotlib`; reserve **`SWE-Gym`'s 11 repos** as a holdout, since they are computationally proven disjoint from SWE-bench and ship both `patch` and `test_patch` for scoring (accepting that 10 of the 11 also appear in SWE-smith-py, so SWE-smith must be filtered to honour the split); and use **`SWE-bench-Live`** as the fresh, continuously refreshed eval set, since its instances all post-date 2024 and it is the only MIT-licensed pool with a credible freshness story. Then verify by `(repo, base_commit)` join across every pair of training and eval corpora — because, as the dependency map above shows, the same instances arrive through four different vendors' trajectory sets.

## Realistic totals: ~80k Python tasks, ~4,000 repos, 0.5–3B assistant tokens

**Distinct Python tasks.** The verified, independently computed figures are: **21,336** nebius V1 instances over **3,468 repos / 2,653 owners**, of which **6,542 over 1,790 repos** are image-backed; **7,243** nebius V2 Python instances over **691 repos / 573 owners**, 100% image-backed; **50,908** SWE-smith-py instances over **131 repos**, of which **39,471 are usable** after dropping empty problem statements; **2,438** SWE-Gym instances over 11 repos; **8,101** R2E-Gym-V1 instances over 13 repos, falling to **6,909** with non-empty statements and **4,962** after Verified-repo decontamination. Adding the 2026 arrivals gives **17,202** gold-patch-validated Scale-SWE instances, **3,688** SWE-bench-Live rows, **716** Python LegoFlow tasks and **7,526** II-Vietnam instances. **The honest executable-Python pool is therefore ≈13,785 nebius instances (V1 image-backed plus all of V2-Python, before an uncomputed V1↔V2 dedup) plus ≈39,471 SWE-smith-py, plus ≈25,000 from Scale-SWE / SWE-Gym / decontaminated R2E-Gym / SWE-bench-Live — on the order of 78,000–82,000 distinct Python tasks**, with the remaining 14,794 nebius V1 rows reachable only by building environments yourself from `install_config` via Nebius's SWE-bench fork.

**Distinct repositories is the scarcer resource and the number that should discipline expectations.** Nebius V1's **3,468** repos dominate; SWE-smith-py adds **131**; R2E-Gym adds **13**; SWE-Gym adds effectively **1** (only `bokeh/bokeh` is unique, the other 10 appear in SWE-smith-py); SWE-bench-Live adds ~164. Nebius V2's 691 Python repos were never deduplicated against V1's 3,468, so they may add anywhere from 0 to 691. **A defensible verified range is 3,600–4,400 distinct Python repositories.** The claim that would change this materially is AweAI's — Scale-SWE is said to mine 6M+ PRs across **23,000+ repositories** and to release instances spanning **5,200 repositories** (claim, [Scale-SWE card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AweAI-Team/Scale-SWE/raw/main/README.md)) — but that figure was never verified, and verifying it is the second-highest-value follow-up after the `Open-SWE-Traces` Python filter. **Note how concentrated the synthetic sources are: R2E-Gym-Subset contributes 3,442 SWE-Hero issues across 8 repos, SWE-Gym-Raw 1,397 across 10, SWE-Gym 666 across 1** [verified] — so SWE-Hero's entire repo diversity comes from its SWE-rebench slice, and for a team that already has SWE-rebench, **SWE-Hero adds ~5,505 new issues but only ~19 new repositories.**

**Supervised assistant tokens, separating measurement from extrapolation.** The firmest numbers are the smallest. `SWE-smith-trajectories`' exact SWE-agent-LM-32B recipe (4,954 `xml` trajectories) is **≈106M assistant characters ≈ 30M assistant tokens**; all 11,503 resolved `xml` rows give **≈71M**; every `xml` row including failures **≈160M**; every `tool` row **≈172M**; the full 49,897-traj_id union **order 300M**, but mixing three incompatible action serializations. `R2EGym-SFT-Trajectories` was fully scanned: 3,231 rows, mean 47,294 total and **11,077 assistant characters per trajectory ≈ 10M assistant tokens**. `SWE-Gym/OpenHands-SFT-Trajectories`' 491 rows come to **~2M** — small enough that SWE-Gym's value is as an environment, not a corpus. `SWE-Hero` measures at **45,445 assistant characters per trajectory** over one of fourteen shards, extrapolating to **≈1.56 × 10⁹ assistant characters ≈ 0.39–0.47B assistant tokens**, with full-sequence volume ≈4.8 × 10⁹ characters ≈ **1.2–1.4B total context tokens** — consistent with the viewer's 8.49 GB in-memory figure. Scaling that same per-row rate by in-memory bytes per row gives **SWE-Zero ≈1.6–1.9B**, **`Open-SWE-Traces` ≈12–15B**, and **`Nemotron-SFT-SWE-v3` ≈1–1.5B** — **all three are extrapolations from a single SWE-Hero shard and must not be treated as publisher figures.** `AlienKevin/SWE-ZERO-12M-trajectories` claims **111.06B tokens** across 12,290,800 rollouts (claim). **The nebius OpenHands trajectory set's token volume was never measured at all** — the rate limit hit before the turn-count query completed — which is a conspicuous hole given it is rank 2 on the shortlist and claims the longest average trajectories of any peer set.

**The synthesis a planner should budget against: a Python-only, success-filtered, repository-disjoint, single-serialization blend has a verified floor near 0.5B supervised assistant tokens** (SWE-Hero's measured ~0.4B plus SWE-smith `xml` resolved ~71M plus R2E-Gym ~10M) **and a realistic ceiling of 1–3B** once `Open-SWE-Traces` is filtered to Python and `resolved=1`. Distinct issues carrying trajectories land at **roughly 25,000–35,000 Python issues**, since `Open-SWE-Traces`' per-version Python PR columns top out around 20,000–24,000 and are heavily shared across versions — the card itself warns "PR counts are non-unique across agent/model columns and cannot be summed horizontally". **Token volume is not the binding constraint. Compute and repository diversity are.**

**Software gap.** No official Runpod template ships the pinned stack. The newest official PyTorch is **2.9.0** (`a9dk3g7cny`, a *cluster* template on `cu1300-torch291-ubuntu2404`); the newest non-cluster is **2.8.0** (`runpod-torch-v280`). **PyTorch 2.10, transformers 5.13.1, peft 0.21.2 and compressed-tensors 0.15.0.1 appear in no Runpod template, guide or doc** — treat the whole stack as bring-your-own. The 2.8.0/2.9.0 image tags carry **no `py3.x` component at all**, so their Python version is **not readable from any tool in this session**; Ubuntu 24.04's system Python being 3.12 is general knowledge, not a verified read, and must be confirmed on-pod. Installing transformers/peft/compressed-tensors on top of a stock template is the documented, low-risk path, but **upgrading torch itself from 2.8/2.9 to 2.10 inside a template replaces the image's load-bearing layer and pulls a different CUDA wheel set** — that is the dependency fight the guides steer away from, so a built image based on `runpod/pytorch:1.0.7-cu1300-torch291-ubuntu2404-cluster` (keeping `CMD ["/start.sh"]` so SSH still comes up) is the honest alternative. A public Docker Hub image needs no registry credential; a private one needs `create-registry` first, since `list-registries` is empty. On CUDA pinning, `allowedCudaVersions` and a non-empty `minCudaVersion` cannot both be sent (400), and since every available ≥80 GB card reports only CUDA 13.2, **a `minCudaVersion: "12.8"` floor places fine while an `allowedCudaVersions: ["12.8"]` pin would 400 on capacity.**

**Operational notes that bite.** Getting the checkpoint onto the volume is cheapest over the **S3-compatible API with no pod running** (bucket name = the volume id, `--endpoint-url https://s3api-<DC>.runpod.io/`, credentials being the Runpod user id plus a console-only `rps_...` S3 API key shown exactly once), but **single-object `PUT` caps at 500 MB so multipart is mandatory above that**, and `aws s3 sync` degrades past ~10,000 files or ~10 GB. Pulling directly from the account's existing R2 bucket from inside the pod is the better fit here — with one trap: **environment variables set at create time reach the container's main process but not SSH login shells**, so `$R2_ENDPOINT_URL` reads empty over SSH unless re-exported. Point `HF_HOME`, `UV_CACHE_DIR` and `PIP_CACHE_DIR` at `/workspace` so a restart does not re-download. For a 10–72 hour run use `tmux` (**not preinstalled**; `apt-get install -y tmux`) or `setsid … < /dev/null &` with the log on the volume — a plain `&` dies on SIGHUP when the SSH channel closes — and never the browser web terminal. **REST v2 has no auto-stop field**, so build the stop into the training script's exit path or create the pod with `runpodctl --stop-after`.

**Compute gaps.** **Spot and savings-plan pricing are not exposed by any of these tools** — the v2 catalog returns only `secure`, `community` and `serverless`, so any spot figure must be marked unread (and savings plans are deprecated and no longer purchasable). **The account's credit balance and auto-pay state are unreadable here**; given that pods stop at $0 balance and a volume can eventually be *terminated with unrecoverable data* if the balance stays there, **check the balance in the console before starting a 72-hour run**. The **HIGH_PERFORMANCE per-GB rate has no authoritative public figure** — the docs say it "varies by data center… Check the volume creation flow in the console", and a Runpod blog's "$0.14/GB/mo" is contradicted by the docs themselves; **do not budget on it**. Per-pod cost attribution for the 2026-10-21 spike was not drilled.

## Conclusion

The decisive insight is that the 2026 trajectory boom did not expand the task supply — it expanded the number of vendors rolling out agents over the *same* few thousand repositories. `Open-SWE-Traces` draws only on Scale-SWE and SWE-rebench-V2; SWE-Hero is half SWE-rebench with 100% exact id matches; SWE-Lego, SWE-Review-Traj, SWE-ZERO-12M and mini-coder-400k each sit on one upstream pool. Sixteen million published rollouts therefore resolve to perhaps 25,000–35,000 distinct Python issues and 3,600–4,400 distinct repositories, and the overwhelming majority of that repository diversity traces back to one artifact pinned at `89cdfbab4ab1bd8f5a658bb212d1b63624f4f881`. **The practical consequence is that the marginal value of a second trajectory corpus is teacher and scaffold diversity, not coverage — and that a repository-disjoint holdout is cheap to construct precisely because so few repos are involved, but easy to accidentally violate because the same instances arrive through four different vendors' files.** Deduplicate on `(repo, base_commit)` before you deduplicate on anything else.

Two asymmetries should reshape how the team allocates effort. First, **the diligence bottleneck is legal, not technical**: the data is abundant, permissively carded, and in several cases ships a per-row SPDX licence that makes compliance tractable — but no publisher has audited the 155-plus upstream repositories whose code appears verbatim in patches and images, nobody addresses teacher-provider output terms, and the single most attractive new pool (AweAI's 20,181 executable Python instances with per-instance images) has an **empty licence field** with the grant stated only in card prose. A week of licence enumeration and a direct request to AweAI and Lego-X buys more than another 10 GB of trajectories. Second, **the published downstream results argue against trusting volume**: Nebius's own RFT checkpoints gained **+0.3pp at 30B and actually regressed at 235B relative to the teacher** on SWE-bench Verified, while `Lego-X`'s controlled ablation puts a 1,000-sample LegoFlow SFT **+5.8 Verified points over the identical recipe on SWE-rebench-V2 instances**, and `AlienKevin`'s card reports that keeping failures beat success-only filtering **12.4% to 5.06%**. Instance quality, failure inclusion, and scaffold match appear to dominate corpus size — which makes the ~0.5B verified-token, success-plus-failure, single-serialization blend described above a more defensible starting point than the 12-billion-token ceiling, and makes closing the three open measurements (`Open-SWE-Traces` Python-and-resolved counts, the nebius V1↔V2 Python dedup, and Scale-SWE's 5,200-repo claim) worth more than any additional download.
